My affordance field algorithm maps where the acoustic surface of unaccompanied vocal polyphony invites sustained pitch perception over time. Rather than reducing the sound to discrete fundamental-frequency tracks at the outset, it models how a listener's ear sifts a complex vocal texture into places that can support a heard pitch. The signal is first mapped onto a perceptual frequency scale where simultaneous masking is taken into account, quieting frequencies that are overwhelmed by louder neighbors close by. Across this surface, the algorithm tracks five interacting qualities of the sound: immediate acoustic presence, sustained duration across moments in time, smooth melodic continuity across neighboring pitches, articulatory change that favors fresh onsets over releases, and harmonicity that reinforces pitches supported by upper overtones while reigning in subharmonic shadows when a lower fundamental dominates. Blended together and gently smoothed over time, these strands bring forward the clearest regions of perceptual support while letting background wash recede, showing where emergent pitches and spectral fission are genuinely invited by the singing itself.
