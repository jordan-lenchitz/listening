my affordance prediction algorithm produces a probabilistic representation of affordances of fundamental frequencies over time. it is based on the multi-stage perceptual model i adapt from kahneman (1976) in chapter 2.3. the algorithm begins by implementing the yin pitch estimator on 30 millisecond frames. this provides rapid fundamental-frequency hypotheses together with confidence coefficients and represents the earliest stages of auditory perception. in the subsequent “successive sifting” stage, each frame is evaluated within a bayesian filter defined on a logarithmically spaced frequency lattice so integer partials reinforce while subharmonic artefacts are attenuated. to sharpen the picture, a continuous morlet wavelet transform supplies a long-span, high-definition contour of spectral energy. ridges extracted from the synchrosqueezed surface then furnish a third and final source of prior information: a narrow log-gaussian kernel centered on the principal ridge frequency. the composite triadic prior models the interplay between immediate auditory judgement, “successive sifting,” and conditioned memory. in addition, the filter traces divergence between successive priors and posteriors to quantify moments in which incoming acoustic evidence challenges established expectations. once posterior distributions have been obtained for each frame, peak extraction combined with the munkres assignment algorithm link statistically salient frequencies into continuous trajectories. these trajectories maintain explicit gaps over rests and unvoiced consonants, preserving a distinction between actual silence and predictive uncertainty.
